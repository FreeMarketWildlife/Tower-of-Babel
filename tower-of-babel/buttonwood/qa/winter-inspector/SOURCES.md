# v80 sky and fact provenance

Retrieved October 10, 2026. Facts are short original paraphrases, not quotations.

## Astronomy data

- [Yale Bright Star Catalogue, fifth revised edition (CDS V/50)](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/cat/V/50): named entries with Vmag < 4.5; J2000 right ascension, declination, magnitude and spectral type. Generic color/temperature facts derive from spectral classes O/B/A/F/G/K/M. Bayer/Flamsteed designations identify stars without common names.
- [JPL Horizons API](https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/horizons.html): observer ephemeris, geocentric center `500@399`, ICRF/J2000, quantities `1,9`, snapshot `2027-01-15 12:00 UTC` (21:00 JST). Targets 199/299/499/599/699 supply Mercury/Venus/Mars/Jupiter/Saturn. Coordinates are frozen across the compressed winter night; horizon direction still rotates with local sidereal time.
- [NAOJ January 2027 winter sky and planets](https://www.nao.ac.jp/astro/sky/2027/01.html): cross-check evening Saturn, late-evening eastern Jupiter/Mars, dawn Venus. NAOJ's published chart is for Tokyo; this implementation independently projects coordinates for Sapporo, not Tokyo.
- [NASA winter asterisms](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/skywatching/what-are-asterisms/): Winter Triangle Sirius/Betelgeuse/Procyon.
- [NASA Orion](https://science.nasa.gov/asset/hubble/orion-constellation/): Rigel, Betelgeuse, Belt and Horsehead region.
- [NASA Polaris and the north celestial pole](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/skywatching/what-is-the-north-star/): direction/pole fact.
- [NASA Betelgeuse](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/what-is-betelgeuse-inside-the-strange-volatile-star/): red supergiant.
- [NASA planet facts](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/planets/): Mercury size, Venus heat, Mars rusty surface, Jupiter storms, Saturn icy rings.

Scope: a warm pixel-art horizon panorama of Hokkaido winter, not a planetarium.
No refraction, proper motion, atmospheric extinction or terrain-specific horizon
survey. Precession corrects the catalogue epoch. The Sun and Moon retain the
existing game trajectories and eight-night lunar phases; they are not date-matched
ephemeris claims. Spectral color is stylized into the named Buttonwood palette.

- [NASA Gemini](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/skywatching/night-sky-network/gemini-constellation/): Castor six-star system and Pollux.
- [NASA Procyon B](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/types/): white-dwarf companion.
- [NASA Algol](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/skywatching/night-sky-network/spooky-stargazing/): eclipses and brightness changes.
